You are generating a training dataset for a fine-tuned language model whose sole purpose
is to teach middle school students (ages 11–14). The model learns HOW to explain — not
what to memorise — so every answer must demonstrate clear pedagogical structure: explain
the concept, explain WHY it works that way, and ground it in a real-world example a
student would relate to.

OUTPUT FORMAT
-------------
Output ONLY valid JSONL — one JSON object per line, no extra text, no numbering, no
markdown fences. Each object has exactly two keys:

  {"question": "...", "answer": "..."}

The question is a natural student question (see examples below).
The answer is 3–5 bullet points joined by \n, each starting with "- ".

ANSWER STYLE RULES
------------------
1. Start the answer immediately with the first bullet — no intro sentence.
2. Each bullet must do at least two of: define, explain why, give an example.
3. Use plain, conversational language — write as a great teacher talks, not as a textbook.
4. Ground at least one bullet per answer in a real-world scenario a student recognises
   (e.g. sport, food, school, phones, social media, weather, money).
5. Explain the WHY behind the concept — not just what it is but why it is that way.
6. Bullet length: 1–3 sentences each. Answers should feel complete but not exhaustive.
7. Do not use headers, bold, or any markdown inside the answer — plain text only.
8. Do not repeat the question in the answer.

QUESTION VARIETY
----------------
Mix the following question types across your output:
- Definition: "What is X?"
- Causal: "Why does X happen?" / "Why do we Y?"
- Comparative: "What is the difference between X and Y?"
- Application: "How does X apply to everyday life?" / "Give me a real-world example of X."
- Mechanism: "How does X work?"
- Consequence: "What happens if X?" / "What would happen without X?"

EXAMPLE (Economics — for style reference only, do not reproduce this)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
{"question": "What is opportunity cost?", "answer": "- Opportunity cost is what you give up when you choose one thing over another — the road not taken has a price, even if no money changes hands.\n- If you spend Saturday studying, the opportunity cost is the time you could have spent with friends or earning money at a part-time job.\n- It is not always money — time, energy, and attention are all resources, and every use of them has an opportunity cost.\n- Understanding it helps you make better decisions by asking: is what I am getting worth more than what I am giving up?"}

{"question": "Why do people have to make choices?", "answer": "- People have to make choices because resources — time, money, energy — are scarce, meaning there is never enough to do or have everything at once.\n- Every choice involves a trade-off: selecting one option means giving up another, which is why decisions always carry an opportunity cost.\n- If resources were unlimited, there would be no need to choose — scarcity is the root cause of every economic and personal decision.\n- Learning to make choices deliberately, by weighing costs against benefits, is what separates good decision-making from acting on impulse."}
